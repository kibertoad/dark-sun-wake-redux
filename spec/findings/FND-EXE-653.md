### Added handler's local callback boundary

The bounded source reading agrees with FND-PARTY-293's entry and IRET
boundary. It saves registers, selects DS=57E0 and snapshots DS:3925/3927
into a four-byte SS-relative local. Pair 0000:0001 skips callback dispatch
and reaches restoration. A zero pair or FFFF:FFFF calls the exit wrapper
1000:03EE with one; only if that call returns does its local continuation
fall through to the following work. It does not privately replace the
saved pair with a known valid callback after this request.

The callback continuation clears high then low words DS:3927/3925,
passes the then-current saved vector pair DS:A4DA/A4DC and vector 23 to
1000:04A3, then calls through its then-current local pair at 2C46 with word two.
It does not check the vector-setter result before that call. Its remaining
suffix restores the saved registers and uses IRET. This is neither an
ordinary far-return helper nor a new successful native-exit observation.

The dispatch pair is a callback target, not a pointer the own body reads
as header data. The two explicit signal-slot clears are above the resident
header envelope under the stated DS/model mapping. Actual SS/local/return
storage, callee preservation, callback contents, native vector behavior and
reached callback effects remain unresolved. The initial zero slot or the
known registrations of other signals alone cannot establish this root's
actual admitted state or all possible target writers.

None of the fifty sites lacks a local mechanism reading after this
reconciliation. They are still unresolved in the engine for its explicit
model/declaration reasons. Q-EXE-037 now concerns actual target histories
and their header-relevant effects, rather than reclassifying these fifty
local mechanisms. Q-EXE-033 retains excluded access/transfer mechanisms;
Q-EXE-035/036 retain destination and address-domain obligations, and
Q-EXE-034 the general return/native frame contract.

For next target-effect work, reuse the known header-mutating manager
interfaces of FND-EXE-582 and the window/application-frame target sets of
FND-EXE-587/588. External XMS/saved-vector effects and the loaded-driver
path remain explicit classes, not an unspecified tail of fifty unread
functions. Keep their address/target/native dependencies within the claims
they can change; unrelated output or I/O success is not newly required.
No whole-entry complete reading, queue closure or parity change follows.

## Alternatives

- Fifty unresolved sites mean fifty unread functions. The exact disjoint
  correspondence above and the cited conditional readings refute that count.
- An old forty-nine-site report contradicts the current fifty-site model.
  The later root/control addition and its extra callback explain the change.
- A nominally skipped call can be removed from every model or actual history.
  Its caller/writer/construction exclusions still apply, and roots/assumed
  continuations are not proof of native nonexecution.
- A classified XMS, driver or saved-vector call preserves the headers.
  Classification alone proves neither destination disjointness nor native
  service behavior, target lifetime or restoration.
- Reaching the handler's callback after an exit request proves native return.
  This model deliberately assumes continuations; source ordering is conditional.
